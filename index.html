Measurement framework

From practice questions to responsible measurement.

OpenIQ separates test administration, raw-score calculation, psychometric evidence, and any future norm-referenced interpretation. The scoring pipeline is implemented; IQ conversion remains locked until defensible norms and validation evidence exist.

What the website measures today

  • Correct, incorrect, and unanswered items.
  • Overall raw score and category-level raw scores.
  • Completion count and response record export.
  • Exploratory item statistics from a pilot CSV.
IQ conversion is not enabledA raw score cannot be converted into a valid IQ score without suitable norms and evidence supporting that interpretation. This is a deliberate scientific safeguard.

What must be verified before IQ scoring

  1. A defined construct and controlled administration procedure.
  2. Age-appropriate, sufficiently large and representative norm samples.
  3. Reliability estimates and standard error of measurement.
  4. Validity evidence for the intended use.
  5. Fairness and accessibility analyses.
  6. Independent psychometric review and documented versioning.

Research protocol · Draft for review

Norming and psychometric validation study

This is a proposed study protocol, not evidence that a study has already been conducted. OpenIQ must remain a practice assessment until qualified researchers complete the required review, data collection, analyses, and independent evaluation.

Research ethics gateDo not collect participant data for norming until the protocol, consent process, data-management plan, recruitment materials, and any required institutional ethics review have been approved. Do not collect names, contact details, exact birth dates, or other unnecessary identifiers in assessment datasets.

Stage 1 · Define the construct

  • Specify the intended constructs and target population before collecting data.
  • Write a test blueprint mapping each item to a construct and difficulty level.
  • Commission independent item review for ambiguity, accessibility, cultural and language fairness.
  • Document scoring rules, administration conditions, exclusion rules, and version control.

Stage 2 · Cognitive interviews and usability

  • Ask a small, diverse group to explain how they interpret each item.
  • Identify confusing wording, unintended clues, translation issues, and inaccessible interactions.
  • Revise items before the main pilot; retain a change log and rationale.
  • Do not treat this stage as norming or validation.

Stage 3 · Pilot and item analysis

  • Use a documented recruitment and sampling plan with informed consent.
  • Inspect missingness, item difficulty, item-total relationships, response patterns, and administration effects.
  • Estimate reliability with uncertainty intervals and suitable methods for the test structure.
  • Revise or remove defective items, then test revisions on new data where possible.

Stage 4 · Norming and validation

  • Recruit a sample that represents the explicitly defined target population.
  • Set sample size through a statistical plan, not a convenient fixed number alone.
  • Evaluate validity evidence, measurement precision, subgroup fairness, and possible differential item functioning.
  • Develop age-based norms only if justified by the construct, design, and evidence.

Participant data minimization

  • Collect only variables justified by the research question.
  • Use broad age bands when possible instead of exact dates of birth.
  • Keep consent records separate from pseudonymous response data.
  • Define access, retention, deletion, breach response, and sharing rules before recruitment.
  • Never publish individual-level results or small cells that could identify participants.

Release gates

  1. Gate A: protocol and item blueprint reviewed.
  2. Gate B: ethics and privacy requirements cleared.
  3. Gate C: pilot analysis and item revisions documented.
  4. Gate D: norming and validation evidence independently reviewed.
  5. Gate E: public technical manual published before any norm-based score is released.

Until all relevant gates are met, OpenIQ displays raw practice scores only and does not issue IQ scores or percentiles.

Open methods

Scientific integrity, built into the product.

Statistical calculations can help inspect pilot data. They do not turn an unvalidated item bank into a valid IQ test. OpenIQ separates exploratory research from claims about individuals.

What would a defensible IQ scale require?

A conventional deviation-IQ scale is often expressed as IQ = 100 + 15z, where z = (X − μ) / σ. The difficult part is not the formula: it is obtaining appropriate, representative age-based norms and evidence that the test measures what it claims to measure.

OpenIQ does not calculate IQ because it does not currently have validated norms, reliability evidence, validity evidence, or an independently reviewed standardization study.

Validation roadmap

  1. Define constructs and blueprint the item bank.
  2. Review items for ambiguity, accessibility, and cultural bias.
  3. Pilot with informed consent and a documented sampling plan.
  4. Study item difficulty, discrimination, reliability, and measurement error.
  5. Test validity and fairness across relevant groups.
  6. Conduct independent psychometric review before any norm-based interpretation.

Local pilot-data explorer

Upload a CSV where each row is a participant and each item column contains 1 for correct, 0 for incorrect, and blank for missing. Do not upload names, emails, or other identifying data. Analysis runs in your browser; the file is not sent to a server by this page.

Exploratory statistics are sensitive to sample size, missing data, sampling bias, and item design. KR-20 is an internal-consistency estimate, not proof of validity. Corrected item-total correlations can be unstable in small samples.

Reliability

How consistently does the instrument measure under specified conditions? Report uncertainty and standard error of measurement, not just one coefficient.

Validity

Evidence must support each proposed interpretation and use. A high reliability estimate alone does not establish validity.

Fairness

Language, education, disability access, culture, and test-taking conditions can affect results. These effects must be investigated, not assumed away.

Questions people should be able to ask.

Is this a scientifically validated IQ test?

No. This version is a public practice and research prototype. Its items have not been normed on a representative sample or established as a standardized instrument. It must not be used for hiring, diagnosis, educational placement, or other high-stakes decisions.

Why not show an IQ number?

An IQ score requires a defensible norm group and evidence for the score interpretation. Applying a formula to a small convenience sample or a raw score would create false precision and could mislead people.

Does OpenIQ collect my answers?

This static page has no account or analytics code. Assessment selections and proposals are saved in your browser's local storage. You can export them or reset/delete them. If you host or modify the site, review any added third-party scripts and hosting logs separately.

Can the community edit the live question bank?

Anyone can prepare and export a proposal in this prototype, but local submissions do not automatically reach maintainers or change the published bank. A real community governance process needs a public issue tracker or submission endpoint, moderation, version history, and a documented review policy.

What does “for the people and by the people” mean here?

It means open access, clear limits, understandable methods, privacy-conscious defaults, and a transparent process for reviewing community proposals. It does not mean crowdsourced claims bypass evidence or professional review.
