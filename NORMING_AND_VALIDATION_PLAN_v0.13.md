# OpenIQ v0.13 — Norming and independent validation plan

## Norming is not the same as validation

- **Calibration:** estimate item behavior and refine items using development participants.
- **Reliability/precision:** estimate score consistency and uncertainty under an appropriate design.
- **Norming:** estimate score distributions for a clearly defined target population and age range using a suitable sample and design.
- **Independent validation:** test prespecified score interpretations and agreement on data not used to build or tune the test.

A convenience sample of website users cannot automatically support norms for all Indian adults, all ages, or all language groups. Age bands, language, educational exposure, region, disability/accommodation status and relevant demographic coverage must be defined before recruitment and justified by the intended use.

## Phases and separation

### Phase A — design and governance
Define the intended construct, use, exclusions, languages, age range, administration mode, scoring interpretation, and harms of incorrect results. Obtain psychometric review and determine ethics-review requirements. Confirm licensing and qualified administration requirements for the independent reference assessment.

### Phase B — content and cognitive interviews
Use the content matrix and item-review template. Recruit a small purposive group from the intended population for comprehension interviews (not norms). Revise items based on documented findings.

### Phase C — pilot/calibration
Use a frozen fixed form. Evaluate completion/missingness, floor/ceiling effects, distractor behavior, item difficulty with confidence intervals, timing distributions, and item functioning. Use a psychometrician to choose appropriate classical or item-response methods; avoid interpreting small-sample estimates as stable parameters.

### Phase D — score and reliability study
Predefine the score model and evidence needed for precision. Evaluate suitable internal consistency and test–retest or alternate-form reliability as appropriate. Report standard error of measurement and uncertainty. KR-20 alone does not establish unidimensionality or validity.

### Phase E — norming study
Use a sampling frame that represents the *specific intended population*. Justify sample size and stratification/weighting in advance. Collect only necessary demographics, preserve privacy, report participation and nonresponse, and estimate age-specific distributions with uncertainty. Do not extrapolate beyond sampled age/language/population coverage. Norms are tied to the exact form, administration, scoring and version.

### Phase F — independent confirmation
Use a separate held-out sample; administer an appropriate independent reference assessment using its approved standardized procedure and qualified personnel. Pre-register primary/secondary outcomes, exclusions, missing-data rules, confidence interval, and sample-size calculation before seeing confirmatory results. No post-hoc item edits or exclusions to improve agreement.

## Proposed 95% within ±5 criterion

This is a demanding target and should be evaluated only if both outputs are defensible, comparable IQ-point scores. Primary outcome: proportion with absolute difference ≤5. Proposed success rule: lower bound of the prespecified two-sided 95% confidence interval ≥0.95. Have a statistician/psychometrician calculate sample size from the planned interval/power criterion, plausible true agreement, and attrition. A small software minimum is not a sample-size justification. Also report signed bias, MAE, RMSE, score-range behavior, subgroup uncertainty, and agreement plots; correlation alone is insufficient.
